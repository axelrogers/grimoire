# Salt Line at the Threshold — writing template

The reference spell. Once this one is right, the other eleven get written
against its shape.

**Everything below is yours to write.** Each slot corresponds to a field that
already renders in the app — fill it in and it appears on the success screen
exactly where the dashed red chip is now. Word counts are targets, not rules.

Ward the door against what's unwanted · protection · $11 · glyph ⊕

---

## 1 · Premise — 2–3 sentences (~40 words)

What this working does, said plainly. Not a pitch. The caster has already
paid; this is the first thing they read afterwards, so it should feel like
being told something true rather than sold something.

>

## 2 · What Grimoire did — 1–2 sentences (~30 words)

`performed.text`. Stated at the moment of casting, in the app's own voice.

This is the field that decides whether hybrid reads as substantial or as
filler. It should be specific and true to the practice. If it can't be said
without hedging, that's worth knowing now rather than after launch.

## 6 · After — 1–2 sentences (~25 words)

What to do with what's left. Salt on a floor is a practical problem as well as
a symbolic one; answering it is part of the working being real.

> 

---

## 7 · The hold — one sentence

`hold`. What keeps them in relationship with the working between the cast and
the verdict three days later. It's the retention mechanic hiding inside the
ritual and it costs one line.

Something with a natural end reads better than a timer — a condition that
resolves on its own rather than a countdown.

> 

---

## 8 · The verdict prompt — one question

Fires after three days (`askAfter: "P3D"` — change if the working wants
longer). Spell-specific on purpose: *"Did it work?"* is weak and gets weak
answers. This question is what turns a cast into data the trust layer can
stand on, so it should be answerable honestly by someone who isn't sure.

>
